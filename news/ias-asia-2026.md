        <h1 class="news-article-title">International Astrocyte School Opens Its First Asian Chapter in Nanjing</h1>

        <div class="news-article-copy">
            <p>The inaugural International Astrocyte School – Asian Chapter 2026 (IAS Asia 2026) was successfully held from October 11 to 17, 2026, in Lishui District, Nanjing, China. Under the theme &quot;Glia in Health and Disease,&quot; the School was jointly organized by the International Astrocyte School in collaboration with the Center for Neuroscience and Innovative Drug Development (CNSIDD) of China Pharmaceutical University and CPU Health Industry (Jiangsu) Co., Ltd. Prof. Frank Kirchhoff, Director of CNSIDD, and Prof. Richard Robitaille from the Université de Montréal, Canada, served as Co-Directors of the School. The event received valuable support from China Pharmaceutical University, Nanjing Universal Biotech Co., Ltd. (UNIV), RWD Life Science, Nanjing TRANSVISTA Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (TRANSVISTA), and Azuleon S.a.s. (Padua, Italy). The organizers extend their sincere gratitude to all collaborating and supporting institutions for their contributions to the successful organization of the School. The week-long programme brought together leading scientists and early-career researchers from different countries and regions for intensive scientific training and academic exchange.</p>
            <p>Founded in 2010, the International Astrocyte School (IAS) has long been dedicated to advancing scientific exchange and fostering the development of young researchers in glial cell biology. The 2026 Asian Chapter marked the School's first expansion into Asia, extending its established tradition of sharing cutting-edge knowledge, encouraging open scientific discussions, and promoting close interactions between faculty members and participants. The event provided a valuable opportunity for early-career scientists in Asia to engage directly with internationally recognized researchers.</p>
            <p>The scientific programme featured 14 invited lecturers and covered a broad range of topics, including astrocyte physiology and signaling, glial cell heterogeneity, neuron–glia interactions, neuroinflammation, and neurodegenerative diseases. Keynote lectures were delivered by Prof. C. Justin Lee from the Institute for Basic Science (IBS), Republic of Korea; Prof. Shigeo Okabe from RIKEN, Japan; and Prof. Guanghui Wang from Soochow University, China, highlighting advances in glial research in Korea, Japan, and China, respectively. Invited speakers from the Netherlands, Germany, Canada, Portugal, Japan, the Republic of Korea, and China presented current scientific questions, advanced methodologies, and recent findings in glial biology.</p>
            <p>Prof. Frank Kirchhoff, Director of CNSIDD, delivered lectures on genetic mouse models for investigating astrocyte physiology and the involvement of astroglial GABA_B receptors in epilepsy-related mechanisms. Prof. Xianshu Bai from CNSIDD presented research on oligodendrocyte lineage cells in health and disease, as well as the interactions between oligodendrocyte precursor cells and neurons and their implications for brain function. These presentations highlighted ongoing research at the Center in glial cell biology and related neurological disorders.</p>
            <p>In addition to invited lectures, the programme included oral presentations by early-career researchers, thematic discussions, and interactive Agora sessions. Young scientists from different research institutions presented their findings on astrocyte signaling, neuron–glia interactions, and disease-related mechanisms. They also engaged in discussions with faculty members on experimental design, research methodologies, and scientific challenges. The combination of focused lectures and interactive sessions created an engaging environment for exchanging ideas and fostering scientific connections.</p>
            <p>On October 15, during the School, a group of participating faculty members and students visited the Jiangning Campus of China Pharmaceutical University for an academic visit. The delegation toured the Center for Neuroscience and Innovative Drug Development, the University's Medicinal Botanical Garden, and the State Key Laboratory of Natural Medicines. The visit provided an opportunity to learn about the University's research facilities and ongoing activities in neuroscience, pharmaceutical sciences, and innovative drug development. It also strengthened mutual understanding and created opportunities for future academic exchanges and research collaboration.</p>
            <p>As the first Asian edition of the International Astrocyte School, IAS Asia 2026 provided an important platform for scientific exchange between international experts and early-career researchers in glial cell biology. Through its active involvement in the organization and scientific programme, CNSIDD further strengthened its connections with the international neuroscience community. Looking ahead, the Center will continue to promote international academic exchange, talent development, and collaborative research in neuroscience and innovative drug development.</p>
