        <div class="news-article-copy">
            <p>On October 22, 2026, the 7th Xingyao Qizhi Lecture and the 34th Yaoyuan Academic Seminar, jointly organized by Mudi Meng Honors College and the Center for Neuroscience and Innovative Drug Development at China Pharmaceutical University (CPU), were held at the B4 Lecture Hall in Wenjingyuan, B Group Complex. Professor Frank Kirchhoff, a member of Academia Europaea, Director of the Center for Neuroscience and Innovative Drug Development (CNSIDD) at CPU, and a university professor, delivered a lecture entitled “How Biological Sex Shapes Brain Structure and Function: The Role of Neuron-Glia Communication.” The lecture explored how biological sex influences brain structure and function, highlighting the role of neuron–glia communication and sharing research advances and emerging perspectives in neuroscience. Faculty members and students attended the event.</p>
            <p>Professor Frank Kirchhoff is an internationally renowned neuroscientist and a W3 Professor at the Faculty of Medicine, Saarland University, Germany. He previously served as President of the German Neuroscience Society. His research has long focused on neuron–glia interactions and the mechanisms underlying brain disorders, with major contributions to glial cell physiology, neuroinflammation, brain injury repair, chronic pain, and sex-specific neurobiology. His team has also conducted extensive research on genetically modified animal models and advanced in vivo imaging techniques. His findings have been published in leading international journals, including Science, Neuron, and Developmental Cell.</p>
            <p>The lecture focused on how biological sex shapes brain structure and function, with neuron–glia communication as its central theme. Drawing on his team's research, Professor Kirchhoff discussed the characteristics and potential mechanisms of brain function regulation in different biological sex contexts. He explained how interactions between neurons and glial cells contribute to the regulation of brain function and the maintenance of brain homeostasis, and highlighted the importance of considering sex as a biological variable when investigating susceptibility to neurological disorders and disease progression. By examining brain function through the lens of neuron–glia interactions, the lecture offered valuable perspectives on understanding neurological disorders and exploring potential therapeutic strategies.</p>
            <p>During the discussion session, faculty members and students raised questions about glia-targeted drug development, the establishment of animal models for investigating sex differences, and the application of in vivo imaging techniques. Drawing on his research experience, Professor Kirchhoff addressed these questions and exchanged insights with the audience on research strategies, experimental design, and the consideration of sex as a biological variable in neuroscience. The discussion deepened participants' understanding of current approaches and emerging directions in neuroscience and encouraged further interdisciplinary exploration at the interface of basic neurobiology and pharmaceutical research.</p>
            <p>The lecture highlighted advances in neuron–glia interactions and sex-specific neurobiology, furthering participants' understanding of brain function and neurological disease research. As an important platform for neuroscience research and original drug discovery at China Pharmaceutical University, the Center for Neuroscience and Innovative Drug Development will continue to leverage its international academic collaborations, promote frontier scientific exchange and interdisciplinary integration, and strengthen connections between basic neuroscience and drug discovery. Through these efforts, the Center aims to advance scientific innovation and support talent development in related fields.</p>
