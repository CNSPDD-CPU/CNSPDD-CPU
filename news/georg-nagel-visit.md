            <p>On October 21, 2026, as China Pharmaceutical University celebrated its 90th anniversary, Professor Georg Nagel of the University of Würzburg, Germany, a 2026 Nobel Prize laureate in Physiology or Medicine, visited the Center for Neuroscience and Innovative Drug Development (CNSIDD) at China Pharmaceutical University. He met with the center’s principal investigators and students for academic exchange. Professor Nagel subsequently visited the university’s medicinal plant garden to learn about its research infrastructure and distinctive scientific resources.</p>
            <p>That afternoon, Professor Nagel delivered a lecture entitled From bacteriorhodopsin via channelrhodopsin to optogenetics at the inaugural event of the Master Forum for Innovating Drug, a special Nobel lecture held at the gymnasium on the university’s Jiangning Campus. Professor Haiping Hao, President of China Pharmaceutical University, delivered the welcome remarks. Professor Frank Kirchhoff, Member of the European Academy of Sciences and Director of the Center for Neuroscience and Innovative Drug Development at China Pharmaceutical University, introduced the speaker and chaired the academic exchange.</p>
            <p>In his lecture, Professor Nagel began with the structure and light-response mechanisms of rhodopsins, tracing the discovery of channelrhodopsins from bacteriorhodopsin. He introduced early studies of light-driven proton transport using Xenopus laevis oocytes and electrophysiological techniques, and highlighted the discovery and characterization of ChR1 and ChR2 in collaboration with Professor Peter Hegemann. These studies revealed the light-gated ion channel properties of channelrhodopsins, laying the molecular foundation for the optical control of neuronal activity.</p>
            <p>Professor Nagel also discussed applications of optogenetics in model organisms, including Caenorhabditis elegans and fruit flies, as well as emerging research into visual restoration, experimental interventions for epilepsy, and the optical control of pollen tube growth. These examples demonstrated the potential of optogenetics across different fields.</p>
            <p>During the discussion, faculty members and students raised questions about the applications of scientific discoveries, the selection of research directions, and interdisciplinary collaboration. Professor Nagel explained that his research originated from an interest in the mechanisms of ion transport across biological membranes, without a predefined application goal. He encouraged young researchers to remain curious, explore scientific questions that interest them, and value mentorship, academic collaboration, and teamwork.</p>
            <p>Following the lecture, Professor Haiping Hao presented Professor Nagel with a commemorative certificate for the Master Forum for Innovating Drug. Professor Nagel subsequently visited the State Key Laboratory of Multi-target Natural Medicines.</p>
            <p>The academic exchange provided faculty members and students at CNSIDD with an opportunity to learn about key scientific discoveries in optogenetics and their development, while offering inspiration for formulating research questions, broadening interdisciplinary perspectives, and exploring innovative drug discovery.</p>
