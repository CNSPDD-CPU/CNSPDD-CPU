            <p>On October 10, 2026, the Center for Neuroscience and Innovative Drug Development (CNSIDD) at China Pharmaceutical University (CPU) held an academic symposium entitled &quot;Brain Homeostasis Beyond Neurons: Glia, Immunity and Metabolism&quot; at the Jiangning Campus. The event brought together researchers from Saarland University, University Medical Center Utrecht, Université de Montréal, the University of Porto, Nanjing University, and China Pharmaceutical University to exchange recent advances in glial biology, neuroimmune regulation, and therapeutic approaches to neurological disorders.</p>
            <p>The morning session began with Professor Frank Kirchhoff, Director of CNSIDD, who presented research on the influence of voltage-gated calcium channels in the oligodendrocyte lineage on sex-specific behavior. Professor Xianshu Bai from China Pharmaceutical University discussed the interactions between oligodendrocyte precursor cells and neurons and their implications for brain function. Professor Elly Hol from University Medical Center Utrecht shared insights into reactive astrocytes in Alzheimer's disease, while Professor Matthew Holt from the University of Porto explored CNS-penetrant adeno-associated viral vectors and their potential applications in targeted biological therapies.</p>
            <p>The afternoon session featured Professor Richard Robitaille from Université de Montréal, who highlighted the neuromuscular junction as a potential therapeutic target and source of biomarkers in amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS). Ke Shuai from Nanjing University discussed the regulatory role of OLFML3 in neuronal and peripheral immune microenvironments. Ziyang Zhang from China Pharmaceutical University presented research on the screening and mechanisms of brain-penetrating compounds targeting Tau fibrils in Alzheimer's disease.</p>
            <p>The symposium addressed key scientific questions related to brain homeostasis and neurological disorders, covering glial cell biology, neuroimmune interactions, disease mechanisms, and innovative therapeutic strategies. Scientific discussions among the participants fostered interdisciplinary exchange and encouraged the integration of diverse research perspectives and approaches.</p>
            <p>The symposium further strengthened academic connections between CNSIDD and universities and research institutions in China and abroad, providing a valuable platform for expanding international collaboration and advancing interdisciplinary research in neuroscience and innovative drug development.</p>
